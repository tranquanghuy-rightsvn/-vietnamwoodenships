Asuka II – an icon of Japanese cruising

Asuka II began life as Crystal Harmony, built by Mitsubishi Heavy Industries at Nagasaki for Crystal Cruises and delivered in 1990. In 2006 she was transferred to the parent company NYK (Nippon Yusen Kaisha) and renamed Asuka II, replacing the first Asuka.

The ship is about 241 m long and nearly 30 m wide, at roughly 50,000 gross tons. For many years Asuka II has been regarded as the largest cruise ship under the Japanese flag, famous for world cruises and voyages around the coasts of Japan. Refined service, elegant interiors and a gleaming white exterior have made her a symbol of premium cruising in Asia.

Model details

Model DL 00012 measures 80 × 11 × 24 cm and is handmade from wood, finished mainly in white like the real ship. Highlights include:

  • A long white hull with a dark waterline and the name ASUKA II at the bow.
  • A multi-deck superstructure with cabin balconies and lifeboats along both sides.
  • An upper deck with a blue pool, sun terraces and a dark funnel aft.
  • A stepped stern with tiers of decks facing the sea.
  • A polished brown wooden base with brass-coloured supports.

To capture the elegance of ASUKA II, our model makers pay special attention to the curve of the bow, the taper of the superstructure and the stepped stern. Balconies, lifeboats and deck fittings are made one by one and fitted by hand. The hull is painted white in several coats, then the stripes and the ship’s name are added by hand, giving a clean, refined look true to Japanese cruise style. On its polished wooden base the model fits naturally into minimalist interiors, and it photographs beautifully for offices and showrooms.
