The batwing sailing boat – a symbol of Ha Long Bay

The model is based on the traditional sailing boat of the fishing communities of Quang Ninh province, known in Vietnamese as the batwing boat or thuyền ba vách ("three-plank boat"). The name comes from the way each side of the hull is built from three large wooden planks, which makes the boat steady in rough water. The sails were dyed with the juice of the củ nâu tuber, giving them their red-brown colour and protecting the fabric from salt air.

What makes this boat special is a sail plan that lets it work to windward: by trimming the sheets, the helmsman sails a zig-zag course against the wind. Since the 1950s the brown sails on the bay have appeared in countless classic photographs and films of Ha Long. Today Quang Ninh is restoring these boats for visitors as a way of preserving the region's maritime heritage.

About the model

This model, code TB 004, measures about 150 × 30 × 120 cm and is handmade in wood at the Vietnam Wooden Ships workshop:

  • A glossy red-brown hull with the gently curved bow and stern of a traditional boat.
  • Two masts carrying golden-brown batwing sails divided by horizontal battens.
  • A deck with a covered cabin and many small, carefully made details.
  • A large oval wooden base that keeps the boat steady on display.

Building a model of this size takes a great deal of handwork. The hull is shaped and finished in several coats so the colour stays deep and even, while each sail is cut from fabric, coloured and fixed to its battens one by one so that the batwing curve looks natural. The result is a piece that reads clearly from across a room and still rewards a close look at its details.
