From working boat to furniture

Around the world, old wooden boats – and new hulls built in the image of real ones – are increasingly turned into furniture: coffee tables, bookcases, bars, even beds. Workshops in Southeast Asia, Indonesia in particular, are well known for reusing boat timber in nautical tables and decor. The appeal is easy to see: a hull has naturally beautiful curves and instantly suggests adventure and holidays by the water.

At this size a boat table is more than furniture; it is a stage for food and drink. The deep hull can hold ice for beer, soft drinks or seafood, while the gunwales make a ledge for glasses, bottles and decoration.

Uses and practical tips

The table suits seafood restaurants, beach bars, resorts, hotels, campsites, outdoor weddings and event booths. When using it as a counter, raise the hull on a plinth or stands so the gunwale sits at roughly 90–110 cm – similar to common kitchen-counter and bar heights – so staff can work comfortably and guests can reach easily.

If you put ice straight into the hull, line it with a waterproof tray or tub and dry the wood straight after the event so it does not stay wet. Outdoors, keep it shaded from sun and rain and bring it inside afterwards. General care advice is in our user guide.
