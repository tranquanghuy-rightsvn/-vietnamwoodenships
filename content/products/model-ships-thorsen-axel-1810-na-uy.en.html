The history of the Axel Thorsen

Axel Thorsen was a gun schooner of the Norwegian navy, built in Trondheim and launched in 1810, during the Napoleonic Wars. She first served as a patrol vessel, escorting merchant ships leaving Arkhangelsk on the northern sea route. In 1816 she was sent north to Finnmark, in the far north of Norway.

What made her famous was a second life in the Arctic. In 1864 she took part in Nordenskiöld's expedition to Svalbard, and she was finally lost in the Arctic Ocean off Novaya Zemlya in August 1872 – more than sixty years after her launch. Sources give her length as roughly 18–19 m and her displacement as about 70 tons.

About the model

This model, code TB 001, measures about 60 × 12 × 55 cm and is handmade in wood at Vietnam Wooden Ships:

  • Two masts with gaff sails and triangular headsails at the bow.
  • A detailed deck with hatches, coiled lines and small fittings.
  • A long bowsprit and hand-tied standing rigging.
  • A light wooden base with two supports.

Small schooners like Axel Thorsen were valued for being fast, handy and able to work in narrow coastal waters, which is why navies of the period used them for patrol and escort duties. On the model this shows in the slim hull, the raked masts and the large fore-and-aft sails, which let a small crew handle the ship. The workshop builds each piece entirely by hand, from the planked hull to the hand-sewn sails and hand-tied rigging, so no two models are exactly alike.
