The original: the French navy schooner La Jacinthe

La Jacinthe was a goélette – a two-masted schooner – of the French navy dating from around 1823 (some sources give 1825). Her design is usually credited to the naval engineer de la Morandière. French fleet records list her in a class of small two-gun schooners built at the Toulon arsenal between 1823 and 1826, together with sister ships such as Jonquille.

Schooners like La Jacinthe handled light duties such as carrying dispatches and patrolling the coast. The ship became famous among model makers thanks to the detailed monograph by French maritime historian Jean Boudriot, with complete plans of the hull, deck and rig. As a result La Jacinthe has been built at many scales and is a classic among small nineteenth-century sailing ships.

About the model

This model, code TB 002, measures about 55 × 12 × 55 cm and is handcrafted in wood at the Vietnam Wooden Ships workshop:

  • A glossy red-brown hull with softly curved bow and stern.
  • Two masts: small square topsails on the foremast, combined with fore-and-aft sails and headsails.
  • Clearly visible hand-tied ratlines and shrouds, and a long bowsprit.
  • Two French flags on the mast and at the stern.
  • A dark wooden base with two turned supports.

Although small, La Jacinthe is built with the same care as our larger ships: the hull is shaped and finished by hand, the sails are cut and sewn individually, and the rigging is tied line by line. The topsail-schooner rig – square sails above, fore-and-aft sails below – gives the model a lively profile that looks good from every angle. Because each piece is handmade, small variations in wood tone and finish make every model one of a kind.
