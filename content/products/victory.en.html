The history of HMS Victory

Designed by Sir Thomas Slade, HMS Victory was launched at Chatham Dockyard on 7 May 1765. She is a first-rate ship of the line rated at 104 guns, spread over several decks: 32-pounders on the lower deck, 24-pounders on the middle deck and 12-pounders above. She was first commissioned for active service in 1778, during the American War of Independence.

Victory entered legend as Admiral Horatio Nelson's flagship at the Battle of Trafalgar in 1805, when the British fleet defeated the combined French and Spanish fleets. Nelson was killed on her deck. Since 1922 she has rested in dry dock at Portsmouth Historic Dockyard, open to visitors. She remains a commissioned ship of the Royal Navy and is the world's oldest naval vessel still in commission.
