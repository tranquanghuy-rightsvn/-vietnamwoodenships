The Wasa model (also spelled Vasa) is a wooden miniature of Sweden's most famous warship. A glossy brown hull, two closely spaced rows of gunports, a carved blue band at the stern and cream canvas sails make it both imposing and rich in detail. Collectors love this ship as much for its extraordinary history as for its looks.

The story of the warship Vasa

Vasa was built on the orders of the Swedish king Gustav II Adolf, who wanted one of the most powerful warships in the Baltic. She carried around 64 guns, most of them on two gun decks. Her hull was about 47.5 m long, and with the bowsprit her overall length is estimated at about 69 m.

On 10 August 1628, on her maiden voyage, Vasa sailed only about 1,300 m out of Stockholm harbour before she heeled over and sank. Researchers attribute the disaster to a ship that was top-heavy, too narrow for her height and short of ballast. She was raised in 1961, conserved, and since 1990 has been displayed in the Vasa Museum on Djurgården in Stockholm. Today she is regarded as the best-preserved seventeenth-century ship in the world.

About the model

This model, code TB001, measures about 80 × 15 × 75 cm and is handcrafted in wood at Vietnam Wooden Ships. Notable details:

  • A warm brown hull with the planking clearly shown along its length.
  • Two rows of dark gunports along the sides, plus decorative work at bow and stern.
  • A blue band at the stern recalling the real ship's famous carvings.
  • Three masts and a bowsprit with cream canvas sails and hand-tied rigging.
  • A wooden base with two saddle-shaped cradles holding the hull securely.

The real Vasa is famous for the hundreds of carved sculptures that once decorated her stern and sides, intended to display the power of the Swedish crown. Our model interprets that richness at a smaller scale with painted decoration, carefully shaped stern galleries and fine trim along the gunwale. Each piece is built entirely by hand, so the colours of the wood and the finish vary slightly from one model to the next, making every Wasa unique.

Display suggestions

Wasa suits a cabinet top, console or wide display shelf with room above for the masts. It looks particularly striking in a classic living room, the study of a history enthusiast or an office reception. Place it at an angle so visitors can see both the broadside and the decorated stern.
