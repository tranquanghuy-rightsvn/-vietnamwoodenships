The Batavia model recreates one of the most famous merchant ships of the seventeenth century – the flagship of the Dutch East India Company (VOC). Her rounded hull and high stern, green upper works with gold trim, red gunports and red-white-blue flags flying from the mastheads give the model an unmistakably old-Dutch character. It is a natural choice for lovers of maritime history and seafaring adventure.

The history of the Batavia

Batavia was built in Amsterdam in 1628 as the VOC's new flagship, intended to carry cargo, silver and passengers on the long route to Asia. She left Texel on her maiden voyage on 29 October 1628. On 4 June 1629 she ran aground and was wrecked on the Houtman Abrolhos islands off Western Australia. What followed was a brutal mutiny among the survivors – one of the darkest and best-known episodes in maritime history.

More than three centuries later, between 1985 and 1995, a full-size reconstruction was built in Lelystad in the Netherlands under master shipwright Willem Vos, using traditional materials such as oak and hemp. The replica is about 56.5 m long and is now a highlight of the Batavialand museum. Part of the original hull was recovered and is displayed in Fremantle, Western Australia.

About the model

This model, code TB 005, measures about 80 × 15 × 75 cm and is handmade in wood at Vietnam Wooden Ships. Up close you will notice:

  • Green upper sides with gold borders, in the decorative style of seventeenth-century Dutch ships.
  • A row of red gunports along the hull and a tall stern with gallery and windows.
  • Three masts plus bowsprit, with sails partly furled so the shrouds and ratlines are clearly visible.
  • Dutch flags at the mastheads and stern that bring the whole piece to life.
  • A long wooden base with three brass supports for a stable display.

Dutch East India ships were known for their sturdy, rounded hulls built to carry as much cargo as possible on voyages lasting many months. On the model you can see this in the full midsection and the high, decorated stern where officers and passengers lived. The craftsmen reproduce these proportions by hand, plank by plank, then add paintwork, gunports and rigging so that the finished piece feels like a ship rather than a toy.

Display suggestions

At 80 cm long and 75 cm high, Batavia needs a cabinet top, console or wide shelf with open space above. Its warm wood and green tones suit a study with wooden furniture, a home library, a hotel lobby or a classic-style restaurant. It is also a gift full of story for partners who love history, especially anyone with ties to the Netherlands or Australia.
