STAR 1 – the bow of the model reads “STAR I” – is a ship with a truly unusual profile: a high, sweeping bow, an A-frame mast up front and a long raised walkway running from the bridge all the way to the bow. These are the hallmarks of a mid-20th-century Norwegian steam whale catcher – a small, fast and powerful vessel built to work in some of the harshest waters on the planet. Vietnam Wooden Ships recreates her in wood with a high level of detail, making this a model for collectors who love maritime history.

The prototype: the Norwegian whale catcher STAR I

According to the ship-history archive of Sandefjord, Norway – once the centre of the world's whaling industry – STAR I was a steam whale catcher completed in 1956. Together with her sister NORTREFF, she was among the largest whale catchers ever built in Norway, and she was the only one with an A-frame foremast, the very feature that stands out on this model.

Catchers of this period usually worked alongside a large factory ship in the Antarctic. Their raised bow helped them handle heavy seas, while the elevated walkway let the gunner move quickly from the bridge to the harpoon gun on the bow without crossing a deck that was often awash. Commercial whaling now belongs to the past, and ships like STAR I survive only in archives, old photographs and museums – which gives their models a special historical value.

Every part is made by hand: the hull is shaped from wood and painted in several coats, while the masts, railings and walkway are assembled piece by piece to keep the proportions true.
