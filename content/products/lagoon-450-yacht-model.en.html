The Lagoon 450 – a French cruising catamaran

Lagoon is a French builder known for roomy sailing catamarans designed for families and charter fleets. The Lagoon 450 was introduced around 2010 and stayed in production for almost ten years in two main versions: the 450 F with a raised flybridge helm and the 450 S with a lower helm station just behind the saloon. The hulls were drawn by the naval architects VPLP (Marc Van Peteghem and Vincent Lauriot Prévost), while the interior was styled by Nauta Design.

The real boat measures roughly 13.96 m (about 46 ft) in length, almost 8 m in beam, and draws only around 1.3 m, which lets her anchor in shallow bays that monohulls cannot reach. The two parallel hulls create a wide, stable living platform that barely heels under sail, which is why the Lagoon 450 became a familiar sight in Caribbean and Mediterranean marinas.

Model details

Model DL015 measures 70 × 40 × 100 cm (length × width × height). It is handmade from wood and painted white throughout, true to the Lagoon livery. Look closely and you will find:

  • Twin hulls with hull windows, joined by a central deck, and stainless-style railings around the bows and sterns.
  • A saloon with a forward windscreen, a hardtop and the helm station above.
  • A tall mast carrying white cloth mainsail and jib, with standing rigging.
  • A tender hanging at the stern, plus winches, seating and a wheel.
  • A reddish-brown wooden base with two metal pedestals for a stable display.

What makes the model special is its proportions. A catamaran is wide and airy rather than long and narrow, so our craftsmen pay close attention to the spacing between the two hulls, the shape of the bridge deck and the angle of the sails. The mast is set exactly on the centreline and the rigging is tensioned so it stays straight for years. The cloth sails are cut and stitched by hand rather than carved from wood, which gives the model a light, realistic look when light passes through them.
