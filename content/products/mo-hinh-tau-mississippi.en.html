Steamboats of the Mississippi

From the early 1800s, steamboats became the backbone of transport on the Mississippi and its major tributaries. One of the first on the western rivers was the New Orleans, built in 1811. To cope with shallow water and shifting sandbars, these boats had flat bottoms and a very shallow draught, with most of their space above the main deck in tiers of passenger cabins.

The sternwheeler gradually won out because its hull was narrower and the paddle wheel was protected at the rear. The tall twin stacks created the draught for the boilers and became the steamboat’s signature. The golden age lasted until railroads spread in the second half of the 19th century, and today riverboats such as the American Queen keep the style alive for travellers.
