Where engineering meets warfare

Before gunpowder became widespread, armies relied on machines that used the tension of bows, twisted ropes or counterweights to hurl arrows, stones and spears over long distances. Once gunpowder reached Europe, cannons cast in bronze or iron and mounted on wooden carriages gradually became central to warfare on land and at sea – on sailing warships, dozens of guns lined each gun deck.

Many Renaissance engineers were fascinated by weapon design. The most famous example is Leonardo da Vinci's “giant crossbow”, sketched around 1482 while he was in Milan and preserved today in the Codex Atlanticus. Historical sources note that Leonardo himself never built it; even so, his design has inspired many wooden models reconstructed since then for museums and collections. The CANNON series from Vietnam Wooden Ships shares that spirit, recreating historic machines in wood so viewers can understand how they worked.
