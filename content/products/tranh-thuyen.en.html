The Boat Painting CN 002 is an 80 × 60 cm relief picture in which the ship is not painted but built in wood like a true model – simply cut lengthways in half and mounted on the panel. This piece depicts a three-masted sailing ship in the style of an old warship: a multi-deck hull with a row of gun ports, a high stern, white canvas sails and rigging running from the sides to the mastheads. It sits on an earthy burlap background inside a black-painted wooden frame.

From the shipwright's half model to wall art

Before technical drawings and computers, shipbuilders often carved a wooden half-hull model to work out the shape of a hull and show the design to the owner. Only half was needed because a hull is symmetrical; measurements taken from the model were scaled up to build the real ship. Once the ship was finished, the model was often fixed to a plank and hung in the yard office or presented to the owner. Today half hulls are mostly decorative nautical pieces, and antique ones with a documented history are sought-after collectibles.

The ship in this picture has the look of the square-rigged warships of the 17th and 18th centuries: three masts, a hull rising toward the stern and a line of gun ports along the side – features typical of galleons and warships of that era.

Where to hang it and how to care for it

It suits living rooms, studies, executive offices, hotel lobbies and seafood or nautical-themed restaurants, and makes a meaningful gift for seafarers, shipping and logistics companies, or anyone who likes the wish for "fair winds". Hang it at eye level, away from direct sun and damp walls; dust it gently with a soft brush rather than a wet cloth so the rigging and sails stay clean and taut.
