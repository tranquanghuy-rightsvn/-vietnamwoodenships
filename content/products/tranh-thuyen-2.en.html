The schooner: a ship for trade and racing

A schooner is a sailing vessel with two or more masts carrying mainly fore-and-aft sails that run along the line of the hull rather than square sails. On a two-masted schooner the mainmast, aft, is usually taller than the foremast. The classic schooner sail is the gaff sail – a four-sided sail whose top edge is laced to an angled spar – set with one or two headsails at the bow and sometimes topsails above.

Fore-and-aft sails are easy to handle, so schooners needed small crews, sailed close to the wind and were fast. The type flourished in colonial North America and peaked in the second half of the 19th century, when thousands of schooners carried cargo and fished along the coasts. Wooden schooners are still kept today as training ships, excursion boats and symbols of seafaring life.
