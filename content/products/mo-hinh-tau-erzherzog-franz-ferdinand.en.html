A paddle steamer named after an archduke

“Erzherzog Franz Ferdinand” means “Archduke Franz Ferdinand”, heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne. In the early 20th century many vessels of the Austro-Hungarian Empire were named after members of the imperial family. A well-known example is the paddle steamer built by Ganz Danubius in 1913 for Danube service, originally named Ferenc Ferdinánd főherceg (the archduke’s name in Hungarian). After the First World War she was renamed Rigó, then Leányfalu, and from 1955 Kossuth. Today the Kossuth is moored on the Pest bank in Budapest as a museum ship telling the story of Danube navigation.

Ships of this type had long steel hulls with a shallow draught, two large side paddle wheels under paddle boxes, a tall central funnel and a covered passenger deck. They were the elegant way to travel and sightsee along the river between Vienna, Budapest and the riverside towns.

Model details

Model DL 012 measures 80 × 15 × 22 cm and is handmade from wood, featuring:

  • A long white hull with a red waterline and the low profile of a river ship.
  • Side paddle boxes lettered ERZHERZOG FRANZ FERDINAND.
  • A central funnel and a covered passenger deck running most of the ship’s length.
  • Rows of saloon windows, railings and flagstaffs fore and aft.
  • A reddish-brown wooden base with two supports.

Every part of the model is shaped by hand: the long hull is planked and sanded smooth, the paddle boxes are built up in layers, and the awning deck, funnel and window frames are fitted one by one. The white paint is applied in thin coats and the lettering is added by hand, so the model looks as crisp as a freshly painted river steamer at the start of the season.
