The story of the real Christian Radich

Christian Radich is a Norwegian full-rigged ship built at the Framnæs shipyard in Sandefjord and delivered in 1937. She is named after a Norwegian businessman whose bequest funded the training of young seafarers, and from the start she served as a floating school for cadets of the Norwegian merchant navy.

Her hull is about 62.5 m long, and with the bowsprit her overall length reaches roughly 73 m. After the upheaval of the Second World War she returned to training duties in 1947. Audiences around the world know her from the 1958 film Windjammer and from the BBC television series The Onedin Line. She is still sailing today, with Oslo as her home port, and is a regular guest at the major European tall-ship festivals.

About the model

This model, code BT 003, measures about 75 × 11 × 65 cm (length × width × height) and is built by hand in wood at the Vietnam Wooden Ships workshop. Details you can see on the piece:

  • A white hull with a deep red bottom separated by a fine blue line – the real ship's signature colour scheme.
  • Three masts carrying many yards of cream square sails, plus headsails at the bow and a fore-and-aft sail on the mizzen.
  • Shrouds, ratlines and running rigging tied by hand, giving the model real depth up close.
  • Deckhouses, lifeboats and railings finished with care.
  • A dark wooden base with two supports that holds the ship securely on display.

Every Christian Radich model starts as a bare wooden hull that is shaped, sanded and painted by hand before the masts are stepped. The yards are fitted one by one and each sail is cut and sewn to size, so the finished ship has the layered, airy silhouette that makes full-riggers so photogenic. Because of this handwork, small variations between pieces are normal – every model is unique.
