The prototype: America's standard C2 freighters

The hull of the model carries the name “AMERICAN FLYER”, a freighter of United States Lines. According to the records of the U.S. Maritime Administration (MARAD), she was a C2-S-B1 cargo ship built for the U.S. Maritime Commission during the Second World War, bought by United States Lines in 1946 and renamed American Flyer. Later in her career she was transferred to the U.S. Navy for ocean survey work. The same company ran other C2 ships with “American” names, such as American Scout, built in 1946 in North Carolina.

The C2 was one of the first standard cargo ship designs developed by the U.S. Maritime Commission at the end of the 1930s. At roughly 140 metres long, it had several holds with large hatches and its own masts and booms, so it could load and unload cargo without shore cranes. Hundreds of C2s were built, serving in war and in peace, and their profile became the familiar image of a 20th-century freighter.
