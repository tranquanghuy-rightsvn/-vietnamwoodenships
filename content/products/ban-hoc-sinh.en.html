Pine or rubberwood – which should you choose?

Both are natural woods widely used in home furniture. Pine is light yellow with bold grain and is light enough to move easily, but it is a softer wood, so the surface dents or scratches more readily under hard knocks. Rubberwood is pale with a fine, even grain and is harder than pine on the Janka scale commonly quoted by furniture makers, so it copes better with daily wear and is more stable in humid conditions.

In short: choose pine if you love a rustic look with strong grain; choose rubberwood if the desk will take daily writing, leaning and heavy school bags. Both are indoor woods and should be kept away from water and direct sun.

Getting the desk and chair heights right

Ergonomic guides for children share one rule: when writing, a child's elbows should be at roughly 90 degrees, with the desktop about 20–30 cm above the seat. Older pupils typically use a desk around 70–76 cm high; for younger children, use an adjustable chair and a footrest so they sit properly. Place the desk near a window but out of direct sun on the work surface, and add a neutral-light desk lamp for evenings.

Wipe the desk with a well-wrung damp cloth, don't leave glasses of water standing on the wood, and avoid harsh cleaners. See our user guide for more care tips.
