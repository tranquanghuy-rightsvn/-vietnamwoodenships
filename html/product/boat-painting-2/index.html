This version of the Boat Painting CN 002 has a lighter, more graceful feel: on an earthy burlap background sits a wooden half hull of a two-masted schooner, painted white above and red below, its white fore-and-aft sails full as if running before the wind. The schooner: a ship for trade and racing

A schooner is a sailing vessel with two or more masts carrying mainly fore-and-aft sails that run along the line of the hull rather than square sails. On a two-masted schooner the mainmast, aft, is usually taller than the foremast. The classic schooner sail is the gaff sail – a four-sided sail whose top edge is laced to an angled spar – set with one or two headsails at the bow and sometimes topsails above.

Fore-and-aft sails are easy to handle, so schooners needed small crews, sailed close to the wind and were fast. The type flourished in colonial North America and peaked in the second half of the 19th century, when thousands of schooners carried cargo and fished along the coasts. Wooden schooners are still kept today as training ships, excursion boats and symbols of seafaring life.

Decorating and care

With its fresh white and red palette, the picture suits light living rooms, Mediterranean-style bedrooms, travel-company offices, beach resorts, and makes a good housewarming or business-opening gift. Hang it beside the three-masted ship painting for a striking pair. Avoid direct sun and damp walls, and dust gently with a soft brush. See our user guide for more.

Each picture is made by hand, so the rigging, the set of the sails and the tone of the wood vary slightly from piece to piece – part of the character of handmade work. Seen side by side, the schooner's low, sleek hull and fore-and-aft sails make an instructive contrast with the tall, square-rigged warships of earlier centuries.
