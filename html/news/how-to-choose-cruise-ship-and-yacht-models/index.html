How to Choose Cruise Ship and Yacht Models

A well-placed cruise ship model or yacht model can become the focal point of an entire room: it evokes long voyages, the calm of the open sea and the patience of the craftsman who built it by hand. But because there are so many styles to choose from, from multi-deck ocean liners and paddle-wheel steamers to modern twin-hull catamarans, many buyers struggle to decide. This guide brings together practical experience to help you choose a wooden ship model that fits your space, suits its purpose and stays beautiful for years.

Why are cruise ship and yacht models so popular?

Unlike historic sailing ships, which carry the look of maritime history, cruise ships and yachts are associated with holidays, exploration and luxury. Stacked decks, long rows of cabin windows, funnels painted in signature colors and sleek white hulls are details that are easy to read and easy to love, and they work in both modern and classic interiors.

For many families, a model ship is a decoration with a story: it recalls a memorable sea cruise, a career connected to rivers and oceans, or simply a lifelong passion for ships. For businesses, a ship symbolizes a journey toward new horizons and "smooth sailing," which is why it is a popular choice for grand openings, gifts of appreciation for partners, or the centerpiece of a reception area.

At the Vietnam Wooden Ships workshop, our cruise ship and yacht models are handmade in wood, from the hull, decks and railings down to small details such as lifeboats and antenna masts. Each model has its own size and character, so understanding your needs before you choose will keep you happy for a long time.

Telling the types of cruise ship and yacht models apart

Before thinking about size or placement, get to know the three main families of designs. Each has its own spirit and suits a different kind of space.

Multi-deck ocean cruise ships

These ships have long hulls, several decks stacked on top of each other, prominent funnels and hundreds of windows. Models in this group are usually long but only moderately tall, which gives them an imposing presence when seen from the side. The ASTOR ship model (80 cm long, 38 cm tall) and the ASUKA ll ship model (80 cm long, 24 cm tall) are typical examples. Asuka II is the name of a well-known Japanese-flagged cruise ship, so this model is especially loved by people who have taken a long ocean voyage.

Steamboats and classic passenger ships

This group recalls the golden age of steam in the 19th and early 20th centuries, when paddle-wheel boats carried passengers along great rivers and ocean liners became symbols of prosperity. The MISSISSIPPI model recreates the style of a Mississippi riverboat with several wooden decks, while the ERZHERZOG FRANZ FERDINAND model has the look of a classic European passenger ship. These models are a natural fit for a study, a home office or a warm, wood-rich interior.

Modern yachts and catamarans

Modern yachts have clean lines, low hulls, wide windows and a sense of private leisure. This group includes motor yachts such as the SILVER DEE yacht model in our modern yachts collection, as well as twin-hull sailing catamarans such as the LAGOON 450 yacht model. Lagoon is a well-known French catamaran brand, and this model stands out with its 40 cm beam and a mast that rises to 100 cm.

Choosing the right size of cruise ship model for your space

The most common mistake when buying a model ship is to look only at photos without measuring the space. An 80 cm model looks compact in a picture, but on a shelf only 25 cm deep it may stick out over the edge, which looks awkward and invites bumps. Follow a few simple rules.

  • Length: the model should take up about two-thirds of the length of the surface it sits on. For an 80 cm model, the shelf or console table should be around 110–120 cm long so there is breathing room at each end.
  • Depth: the surface should be at least 8–10 cm deeper than the model's beam. For a 40 cm wide catamaran such as the LAGOON 450, you will need a table about 50 cm deep.
  • Height: check the clearance above, especially inside a glass cabinet or a multi-tier shelf. Multi-deck cruise ships are usually 22–38 cm tall, while a sailing yacht's mast can reach almost one meter.
  • Eye level: a model looks best when viewed straight on or slightly from above. The ideal surface height is usually between 75 and 110 cm from the floor.
Quick tip: before ordering, use painter's tape to mark a rectangle with the model's exact length and width on the spot where you plan to put it. Leave it for a few days and you will quickly see whether the size feels balanced in the room.

Choosing a yacht or cruise ship model by purpose

Living room decor

The living room is where a model is seen most often, so choose one with a clear silhouette that is recognizable from across the room. A multi-deck cruise ship such as the ASTOR or a long-hulled ship such as the CALLISTA creates a strong horizontal line and looks great on a TV unit or a low sideboard along the wall. If the room has a high ceiling and plenty of open space, a sailing yacht such as the LAGOON 450 placed in a corner adds a vertical accent that balances lower furniture.

Desk and office

On a desk, the model should not eat into your working area. Models 60–70 cm long with a narrow hull, such as the L’AUSTRL ship model (just 9 cm wide) or the BROOM 35 EUROPEAN, are sensible choices. In an executive office you can go for a more imposing model and place it on the cabinet behind the desk, where it looks dignified without getting in the way.

Reception areas, hotels and restaurants

Public spaces need a model large enough to draw the eye, and it should sit in a glass cabinet or acrylic case to keep dust and knocks away. Seaside resorts, hotels and seafood restaurants often choose multi-deck cruise ships to set a holiday mood.

Gifts

A model ship carries the wish for "smooth sailing," which makes it a fitting gift for a grand opening, a promotion, a retirement or a thank-you to a business partner. When choosing a gift, think about the recipient's space: if you are unsure, a 60–70 cm model is always a safe choice because it is easy to place. For people who work in travel or shipping, or who have served at sea, an ocean cruise ship model carries a much more personal meaning.

Matching a model ship with your interior style

Even a beautiful model can look out of place in the wrong setting. Pay attention to the colors of the hull and the dominant tones of the room.

  • Modern, minimalist interiors: choose a white-hulled yacht with clean lines such as the SILVER DEE or the BROOM 35 EUROPEAN. Place it on stone or a neutral-colored shelf and keep the surrounding area uncluttered.
  • Classic interiors with dark wood: riverboats and classic passenger ships such as the MISSISSIPPI or the ERZHERZOG FRANZ FERDINAND blend naturally with wooden bookcases, warm desk lamps and leather.
  • Coastal or resort style: pair a cruise ship model with blue and white tones, rattan, bamboo or light-colored wood.
  • Spaces with plenty of handmade wood: combine the model with wooden furniture in the same spirit – see our guide to handcrafted wooden furniture for your home.

Lighting matters just as much. A strip of warm LED light angled down from above brings out the decks and railings. However, avoid placing the model directly under a powerful spotlight or in direct sunlight, as heat and UV rays can dry out the wood and fade the paint over time.

How to judge the quality of a handmade wooden ship model

Two models with the same name and the same size can differ enormously in quality. Whether you see the product in person or in close-up photos, look carefully at the following points:

  1. Overall proportions: the hull, decks and funnels should be balanced, never unusually squat or tall compared with the real ship.
  2. Wood joints: joints in the hull and decks should be tight, with no glue gaps and no cracks.
  3. Paint and varnish: the surface should be smooth and evenly colored, with crisp, clean edges between colors.
  4. Small details: railings, cabin windows, lifeboats, antenna masts and rigging (on sailing yachts) are where the maker's skill really shows.
  5. Display base: the base should be stable and level, hold the ship securely and not scratch the surface underneath.

Caring for your cruise ship model so it always looks new

Cruise ship models have many small, delicate details, so the right care will keep the piece looking its best for years:

  • Keep it in a dry, well-ventilated place, away from windows with strong sun, direct air-conditioning drafts or damp areas such as bathrooms and kitchens.
  • Dust it regularly with a soft brush or a small air blower; never use wet cloths or cleaning chemicals.
  • When moving it, lift the model with both hands by the base or the hull, and never hold it by the masts, funnels or railings.
  • If possible, display the model in a glass cabinet or acrylic case to limit dust and accidental knocks.

Frequently asked questions

What size of cruise ship model is right for a living room?

In a typical living room, a model 70–90 cm long looks most balanced on a TV unit or low sideboard at least 1.2 m long. If the room is small or you want to place it on a bookshelf, choose a 60–70 cm model.

Should I choose a multi-deck cruise ship or a modern yacht?

A multi-deck cruise ship feels grand and detailed, ideal for spacious or classic interiors. A modern yacht has clean lines and suits minimalist interiors, offices and desks.

Is a yacht model a good corporate gift?

Yes. A model ship carries the wish for smooth sailing and is a popular choice for grand openings, thank-you gifts for partners and anniversaries. A 60–70 cm model fits easily into most offices.

Do I need to keep the model ship in a glass cabinet?

It is not essential, but a glass cabinet or acrylic case limits dust and knocks, and is especially recommended in busy places such as reception areas, restaurants or homes with small children.
